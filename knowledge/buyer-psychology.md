# Buyer Psychology

Used in **State 4 — Buyer Psychology Analysis** of the canonical
workflow, after the Keyword IP Screening gate and before Competition
Analysis. See `workflow/01-canonical-state-machine.md`.

---

## 1. Purpose

Customers do not buy SVG, PNG, DXF, or EPS files. They buy identity,
emotion, belonging, memories, personal expression, solutions, and
creative possibilities. Understanding the buyer is essential before
creating the design — not an afterthought once a design already
exists.

**The core question:** not "what does the design show?" but "what does
this design *mean* to the buyer?"

---

## 2. Six Purchase Motivations

Analyze every product idea through one or more of these:

1. **Identity Expression** — "This represents me." (Dog Mom SVG,
   Teacher SVG, Nurse SVG, Camping SVG, Book Lover SVG.) Design
   implication: emphasize belonging, personality, community,
   recognizable symbols, emotional connection.

2. **Gift Psychology** — "Will this make someone feel special?"
   (Mother gifts, pet memorials, wedding designs, family designs,
   teacher appreciation.) Consider recipient identity, emotional
   meaning, personalization opportunities, memorable symbolism.

3. **Hobby & Passion Psychology** — passionate communities (camping,
   fishing, gardening, baking, reading, crafting, sports, pets) buy
   more readily for things connected to their interest. Look for:
   Community + Shared Symbols + Emotional Connection + Unique
   Expression.

4. **Emotional Connection** — happiness, nostalgia, pride, humor, love,
   remembrance, achievement. Generic: "Dog Paw SVG." Emotional:
   "Forever My Adventure Buddy" — the second connects to a personal
   story, not just a subject.

5. **Aesthetic & Subculture Expression** — "I belong to this visual mood or atmosphere."
   (Cottagecore, Dark Academia, Goblincore, Fairycore, Vintage Botanical, Y2K,
   Coquette, Minimalist Boho.) Buyers are drawn to an aesthetic subculture or interior decor mood — neither tied strictly to a profession ("Teacher") nor an active physical hobby ("Camping"). Design implication: emphasize signature subculture iconography, atmospheric storytelling, specialized palettes, and decorative harmony.

6. **Problem Solving** — some buyers are creators who need a design for
   a shirt, mug, sticker, sign, craft, or gift. The design solves a
   production need, not an identity need.

## 3. Buyer Persona Development

Define a persona before generating concepts.

```
Buyer Persona
Name: Outdoor Family Creator
Age: 30-50
Location: USA
Interest: Camping and family adventures
Purchase Motivation: Create personalized camping merchandise
Emotional Driver: Pride and memories
Preferred Style: Vintage outdoor badge
Buying Trigger: Seasonal camping trips and gifts
```

---

## 4. Micro-Niche Identification

Broad markets are usually saturated. Look for smaller communities
within them.

- Broad: "Dog SVG"
- Micro-niches: "Golden Retriever Mom," "Senior Dog Memorial," "German
  Shepherd Military Family," "Dachshund Coffee Lover"

---

## 5. Identity Layering

Premium designs combine multiple identity layers: **Primary Identity +
Secondary Interest + Emotional Theme + Visual Style = Unique Product
Direction.**

- Basic: "Teacher SVG"
- Layered: "Elementary Teacher" + "Book Lover" + "Vintage Library
  Theme" + "Engraved Badge Style"

## 7. Buyer Language Analysis

Customer language reveals what they actually want. Analyze Etsy
reviews, search phrases, comments, and product descriptions.

- "I need something unique" → avoid generic designs.
- "Perfect gift for my sister" → emphasize gifting context.

---

## 8. How This Feeds Design Strategy

Buyer psychology shapes:
- **Concept** — what idea should exist?
- **Composition** — how should the customer recognize the meaning?
- **Style** — what visual language fits the audience?
- **Messaging** — what words or symbols communicate value?

---

## 9. Buyer Psychology Scoring (State 4, detailed)

```
Identity Connection
Emotional Strength
Gift Potential
Audience Passion
Purchase Motivation
Memorability
```

Example:

```
Identity Connection: 9/10
Emotional Strength: 8/10
Gift Potential: 9/10
Audience Passion: 8/10
Purchase Motivation: 8/10
Memorability: 9/10
Overall Buyer Appeal: 8.5/10
```

**How this relates to later scoring:** this six-dimension breakdown is
the detailed State-4 analysis. It feeds into, but is not the same
number as, **Buyer Appeal** in the Level 1 Opportunity Score
(State 6) or **Buyer Alignment** in the Level 2 Concept Score
(State 9) — see `workflow/03-scoring-architecture.md`. Those later
scores are informed by this analysis; they don't recompute it from
scratch, and they don't need to match this number exactly since they're
evaluating different things (market-level appeal vs. a specific
concept's alignment).

## 11. Rules

- Design for people, not keywords.
- Identify emotional reasons to purchase.
- Search for passionate audiences.
- Create identity-driven concepts.
- Avoid meaningless decoration.
