# IP Risk & Originality

This file covers **originality engineering** — how to actively build
differentiated, defensible concepts. For the actual IP gate mechanics
(the four checkpoints, PASS/MODIFY/BLOCK, retry limits), see
`workflow/02-ip-gates.md` — that content isn't duplicated here.

> **Reminder from the gates file, worth repeating:** IP Safety/Risk and
> Originality are different axes. IP Safety is a legal gate. Originality
> (this file, and the Originality dimension in Concept Score) is a
> craft-quality measure. A concept can be perfectly IP-safe and still
> score low on originality — being legally clear doesn't make something
> interesting.

---

## 1. Philosophy

> Inspiration is allowed. Imitation is not.

Avoiding risk alone is not enough — a design can be perfectly safe and
still be commercially worthless because it's generic. The system must
actively *increase* uniqueness, not just screen out infringement.

> **Etsy Creativity Standards Policy Note (August 11, 2026 Enforcement):**
> Etsy strictly enforces that digital products and items made with
> computerized tools (Cricut, CNC, laser cutters, sublimation) must be
> based on the seller's **own original design**. Listing un-modified
> third-party clipart or pre-made cut files bought elsewhere is no
> longer compliant under "Designed by a seller". Originality
> engineering is mandatory for platform longevity.

---

## 2. Risk Categories (Summary)

Full detail and gate logic: `workflow/02-ip-gates.md`.

1. Trademark risk — brand names, team names, logos, slogans.
2. Copyright risk — copied characters, artwork, recognizable fictional
   designs.
3. Style imitation risk — never prompt "in the style of [living
   artist]"; use technique/characteristic language instead.
4. Franchise association risk — fictional universes, famous characters,
   branded events, mascots.
5. Marketplace similarity risk — not legal, commercial: fully original
   work that's still indistinguishable from thousands of existing
   listings. This is what the rest of this file addresses.

---

## 3. Originality Engineering Framework

Originality comes from combining: **Theme + Audience + Emotion +
Visual Language + Unexpected Elements = Unique Concept.**

---

## 4. Concept Transformation Method

When a popular concept already exists, transform it — don't just alter
it slightly.

**Weak approach:** take "Camping SVG: Mountain + Tree + Campfire" and
change the mountain shape. This is cosmetic, not transformative — it's
still the same concept a hundred other sellers have.

**Strong approach:** transform camping from a generic outdoor symbol
into a nostalgic expedition story, combining vintage cartography,
personal adventure symbolism, wilderness identity, and collectible
badge composition. This changes what the concept *is*, not just what
it looks like.

---

## 5. Four Originality Layers

Premium designs stack multiple layers of originality, not just one:

1. **Concept Layer** — the main idea. E.g. "a retired firefighter
   honoring his career journey."
2. **Symbolic Layer** — meaningful visual elements. E.g. helmet, badge,
   years of service, firehouse symbolism.
3. **Composition Layer** — how elements are arranged. E.g. heritage
   badge, circular engraving, layered storytelling.
4. **Detail Layer** — small unique elements. E.g. hidden symbols,
   texture patterns, secondary storytelling elements.

A concept that only has Layer 1 (a decent idea, generically executed)
will still read as generic. Strong differentiation usually needs at
least three of the four layers working together.

## 6. Originality Score

Evaluated across five dimensions: Concept Uniqueness, Visual
Differentiation, Audience Specificity, Story Depth, Marketplace
Separation.

```
Concept Uniqueness: 9/10
Visual Differentiation: 9/10
Audience Specificity: 8/10
Story Depth: 8/10
Marketplace Separation: 8/10
Originality Score: 8.4/10
```

This is the same **Originality** dimension referenced in Concept Score
(Level 2) — see `workflow/03-scoring-architecture.md`. It is never
merged with IP Safety/Risk; they're evaluated independently.

---

## 7. Balancing Safety and Commercial Potential

Two failure modes to avoid:

**Too safe** → generic designs. A simple heart icon is low-risk and
also low commercial value — safety without originality isn't the goal
by itself.

**Too creative without awareness** → possible IP issues. Originality
pursued without any awareness of the risk categories above can
accidentally wander into unsafe territory.

**The actual goal:** Safe + Original + Commercially Attractive, all
three together — not a tradeoff between them.

---

## 8. Decision Rules

- Identify risky keywords early (State 3, before creative investment).
- Avoid protected references.
- Avoid direct style imitation.
- Avoid copied compositions.
- Increase originality through transformation, not just risk-avoidance.
- Clearly communicate uncertainty rather than guessing silently.
- Recommend safer alternatives when something is flagged.
