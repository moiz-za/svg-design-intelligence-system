# Creative Strategy

Used in **State 7 — Creative Strategy** of the canonical workflow,
after Opportunity Scoring and before Concept Generation. See
`workflow/01-canonical-state-machine.md`.

---

## 1. Purpose

Transform research findings into a structured creative direction
before AI image generation begins. This is the bridge between market
intelligence and creative execution.

Without this stage, AI generation produces random artwork. With it,
generation follows customer intent, market opportunity, originality
strategy, and production requirements — rather than just "make
something that matches this keyword."

---

## 2. The Non-Negotiable Sequence

Never jump directly from Keyword → Generate Image. The correct
sequence:

```
Keyword → Research → Opportunity → Creative Strategy → Concept → Prompt
```

Creative Strategy specifically defines: what should be created, who
it's for, why buyers care, how it should look, and why it's different.

---

## 3. Creative Brief Contents

Every approved opportunity receives a Creative Brief:

```
Product Direction
↓
Target Buyer
↓
Emotional Goal
↓
Design Theme
↓
Visual Language
↓
Differentiation Strategy
↓
SVG Production Requirements
```

---

## 4. Design Positioning

State the product's position explicitly, not just its subject matter.

- Weak: "Cute Cat SVG"
- Strong: "Premium vintage cat lover badge collection designed for
  passionate pet owners who want identity-based merchandise."

The second version tells you who it's for and why they'd buy it. The
first only tells you what's pictured.

---

## 5. Creative Direction Components

**Theme** — the central idea (adventure, nostalgia, humor, elegance,
remembrance, pride).

**Visual Language** — the aesthetic (vintage engraving, modern
minimalism, retro badge, hand-drawn illustration, luxury emblem,
botanical composition).

**Emotional Direction** — the feeling (warmth, pride, belonging, humor,
nostalgia).

**Audience Alignment** — the design must visually communicate to the
intended buyer specifically. A firefighter design shouldn't feel like
a generic flame icon — it should communicate service, honor, identity,
community. Generic execution undercuts even a strong concept.

---

## 6. Differentiation Strategy

Every concept must answer: "Why would someone choose this over hundreds
of alternatives?" Four methods, often combined:

- **Concept differentiation** — a new idea, not a new render. "Dog
  silhouette" → "A vintage explorer badge representing a dog's life
  journey."
- **Audience differentiation** — target a specific group. "Teacher SVG"
  → "Kindergarten teacher personality collection."
- **Style differentiation** — a premium visual approach. "Flat cartoon
  icon" → "Antique engraved illustration with collectible badge
  composition."
- **Story differentiation** — add meaning. "Mountain SVG" → "A symbolic
  mountain representing personal achievement and adventure."

These map directly onto the Originality Layers in
`knowledge/ip-risk-and-originality.md` §5 — use both together rather
than treating them as separate exercises.

---


This document is the direct input to State 8 (Concept Generation) —
